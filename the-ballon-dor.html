The Trophy & Heritage

The Legend of the Ballon d'Or™

Since 1956, the ultimate individual prize in football, blending Parisian haute joaillerie with world football history.

Haute Joaillerie

Crafted by Mellerio dits Meller

Each Ballon d'Or trophy is handcrafted in Paris by the world's oldest jeweler, Mellerio dits Meller (founded in 1613). Requiring over 100 hours of painstaking work by master artisans, the trophy stands 31 cm high and weighs approximately 15 kilograms.

1
The Brass Hemispheres:

Two half-spheres of solid brass are welded together by a goldsmith and shaped using a blowtorch.

2
Chiseling the Seams:

A chiser carves the intricate seam lines and pentagonal panels of a football using a chisel and hammer.

3
18-Carat Gold Bath & Pyrite Base:

The sphere is dipped in a bath of pure 18-carat gold and anchored onto a completely unique chunk of natural pyrite crystal.

The Golden Ball
Height: 31 cm • Weight: 15 kg • Pure Gold Dipped
Official Regulations

The Jury & Three Sacred Criteria

The Ballon d'Or is awarded based on votes cast by an international jury of 100 specialized journalists representing the top 100 nations in the official FIFA rankings (50 for the Ballon d'Or Féminin).

Criterion 1
Individual Performances

Decisiveness in major matches, goals, assists, technical mastery, and impressive character displayed throughout the season.

Criterion 2
Team Achievements

Titles won with club and national team (Champions League, Euro, World Cup, Copa América, domestic leagues).

Criterion 3
Class & Fair Play

Sportsmanship, exemplary on-pitch conduct, dignity in competition, and embodying the highest values of world football.

Points awarded per ballot: 1st (15 pts) • 2nd (12 pts) • 3rd (10 pts) • 4th (8 pts) • 5th (7 pts) ... down to 10th (1 pt)
Chronology

Key Milestones in Ballon d'Or History

1956 — The Birth of the Award

Conceived by Gabriel Hanot and Jacques Ferran, editors of France Football. Stanley Matthews of Blackpool wins the first edition.

1963 — Lev Yashin's Triumph

Dynamo Moscow's 'Black Spider' becomes the first and only goalkeeper to lift the Golden Ball.

1995 — The Global Expansion

Originally restricted to European footballers, the award opens to any nationality playing for a European club. Liberia's George Weah makes history as the first non-European winner.

2007 — Total Worldwide Eligibility

Eligibility expands to all professional players worldwide, and journalists from non-European nations join the voting panel.

2018 — Ballon d'Or Féminin & Kopa Trophy

France Football establishes the women's Ballon d'Or (won by Ada Hegerberg) and the Kopa Trophy for the best player under 21 (won by Kylian Mbappé).

2026 — 70th Anniversary Gala at London Palladium

The historic gala moves to London, England for the very first time in its 70-year history.

The most prestigious individual award in world football, honouring legendary excellence and greatness since 1956.
